Case Study 03 · Infillion Catalina

Using Predictive Modeling to Prioritize Customers

From propensity model outputs to a targeted customer strategy that identified 4× lift among the highest-potential customers.

§ 1The Challenge

The business wanted to identify customers who were most likely to adopt a new product and determine how predictive insights could inform a more focused targeting strategy.

Rather than treating the model output as the end result, I focused on a practical business question:

The questionCan predictive signals help us identify where the strongest opportunity exists?

The goal was to evaluate the model's ability to differentiate customer potential and translate those insights into an actionable customer strategy.

§ 2My Approach
01

Evaluate the Model

I analyzed propensity model outputs across customer segments to understand how effectively the model differentiated customers based on their likelihood of adopting the new product.

Rather than focusing only on overall model performance, I evaluated lift across ranked customer groups to understand whether the model could meaningfully identify higher-potential customers.

02

Identify High-Potential Customers

The analysis showed a clear concentration of potential among the highest-ranked customers.

Customers in the top 10% of the propensity ranking showed approximately 4× lift compared with the broader customer population.

This provided a data-driven basis for prioritizing customers rather than treating the entire population equally.

03

Translate Insights Into Strategy

I translated the model insights into a practical customer targeting strategy, working across Business, Data Science, and Client teams to connect the analytical findings with business priorities.

The key question shifted from:

  • “Who is most likely to adopt?”
  • “Who should we prioritize based on the opportunity?”

This helped bridge the gap between predictive modeling and business decision-making.

04

Inform Targeting

Based on the observed lift and business considerations, the analysis supported a more focused targeting approach.

Rather than broadly targeting the full customer base, the business could prioritize customers where the predictive signal indicated stronger potential.

The model therefore became more than a score — it became an input into customer prioritization and strategy.

§ 3The Impact
4×
Lift among top 10% of customers

The analysis demonstrated that predictive modeling could meaningfully differentiate customer potential and provide a data-driven basis for prioritizing high-opportunity customers.

It also helped translate a technical model output into a business strategy that could be understood and acted upon by non-technical stakeholders.

§ 4What I Took Away

The value of predictive modeling is not simply producing a score. It is using that score to make a better decision. For me, the process was:

Model Output→Lift→Prioritize→Strategy

The most valuable predictive analytics work connects model insights back to a clear business question:

The real questionWhat should we do differently now that we know where the strongest opportunity exists?
