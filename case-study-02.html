Testing What Drives Customer Behavior

From business questions to 100+ experiments that informed customer strategy and contributed to $10M+ in estimated incremental impact.

§ 1The Challenge

The business needed to understand which customer offers, experiences, and strategies were most effective at driving incremental behavior.

Rather than relying on assumptions or evaluating performance after launch, I used experimentation to answer a more fundamental question:

The questionWhat actually changes customer behavior — and by how much?

The goal was to design experiments that could isolate customer response, establish clear measures of success, and turn the results into decisions that could be applied to future programs.

§ 2My Approach
01

Define the Question

I started by understanding the business objective behind each test and translating it into a measurable customer behavior question.

Working with Sales, Ops, and Data Science teams, I helped define:

  • What customer behavior are we trying to change?
  • What would success look like?
  • Which metrics should determine whether the test is successful?
  • What decision will the results help us make?

This ensured that the experiment was designed around a business decision rather than simply measuring performance.

02

Design the Experiment

I translated the business question into an actionable test design, establishing appropriate test and control groups and defining the success metrics upfront.

Depending on the objective, experiments evaluated different customer strategies, offers, and levels of engagement across the customer journey.

I worked closely with cross-functional teams to align on the experiment structure, audience, measurement approach, and operational requirements before launch.

03

Measure Customer Response

Once the experiments were live, I analyzed customer behavior across test and control groups to quantify the impact of each strategy.

I evaluated changes in key outcomes and customer response to understand:

  • Did the treatment change behavior?
  • How large was the observed lift?
  • Which customer groups responded differently?
  • Was the result meaningful enough to inform future decisions?

Rather than focusing only on whether a test “won,” I looked at what the results told us about customer behavior.

04

Learn & Inform the Next Decision

The results were translated into recommendations for Sales, Ops, and Product teams.

Successful experiments helped identify strategies worth scaling, while weaker results provided insight into what did not work and where future experiments should focus.

Across 100+ A/B tests, this created an ongoing learning loop:

Hypothesis→Experiment→Measure→Learn→Iterate

The goal was not to run more tests for the sake of testing, but to continuously improve our understanding of what drives customer behavior.

§ 3The Impact

Across more than 100 experiments, the analysis helped inform customer strategies and contributed to $10M+ in estimated incremental impact.

More importantly, experimentation created a repeatable framework for making customer and business decisions based on measured behavior rather than assumptions.

§ 4What I Took Away

The value of experimentation is not simply knowing whether a test won or lost.

It is understanding why customer behavior changed and using that learning to make the next decision better. For me, effective experimentation connects:

Business Question→Hypothesis→Experiment→Customer Response→Learning→Decision

The strongest experiments don't just answer “Did it work?” They help answer:

The real questionWhat did we learn about our customers, and what should we do next?
